# Event Planning FAQ

## General Event Planning

### Q: How far in advance should I start planning my event?
A: The planning timeline depends on your event type and size:
- **Small events (under 50 people)**: 2-3 months in advance
- **Medium events (50-200 people)**: 4-6 months in advance
- **Large events (200+ people)**: 6-12 months in advance
- **Weddings**: 12-18 months in advance
- **Corporate conferences**: 8-12 months in advance

### Q: What's the first step in event planning?
A: Start by defining your event's purpose and objectives. Ask yourself:
- What is the goal of this event?
- Who is your target audience?
- What's your budget range?
- When do you want to hold the event?
- How many people do you expect to attend?

### Q: How do I create an event budget?
A: Follow these steps for effective budgeting:
1. **Set your total budget** based on available funds
2. **Allocate percentages** to major categories:
   - Venue: 40-50%
   - Catering: 25-35%
   - Entertainment: 10-15%
   - Decorations: 8-10%
   - Photography: 5-8%
   - Miscellaneous: 5-10%
3. **Add a contingency fund** of 10-15% for unexpected costs
4. **Track expenses** throughout the planning process

## Venue Selection

### Q: What should I look for when choosing a venue?
A: Consider these key factors:
- **Capacity**: Ensure it fits your guest count plus 10% buffer
- **Location**: Accessible for your target audience
- **Parking**: Adequate parking or valet service
- **Amenities**: Kitchen facilities, AV equipment, furniture
- **Restrictions**: Catering policies, decoration rules, noise limits
- **Cost**: Rental fees, setup charges, overtime costs
- **Availability**: Your preferred dates and backup options

### Q: Should I visit venues in person?
A: Absolutely! Always visit potential venues in person to:
- See the actual space and layout
- Test acoustics and lighting
- Meet the venue coordinator
- Ask detailed questions about policies
- Take photos and measurements
- Get a feel for the atmosphere

## Catering and Food Service

### Q: How do I choose between different catering styles?
A: Consider your event type and guest preferences:
- **Plated dinner**: Formal events, seated dining, higher cost
- **Buffet style**: Casual events, variety of options, cost-effective
- **Cocktail reception**: Networking events, finger foods, flexible
- **Family style**: Intimate gatherings, shared dishes, interactive
- **Food stations**: Large events, themed options, guest movement

### Q: How do I handle dietary restrictions?
A: Plan ahead for dietary needs:
1. **Ask about restrictions** on invitations or registration
2. **Common restrictions** to consider: vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, kosher, halal, allergies
3. **Work with caterer** to provide suitable alternatives
4. **Label food items** clearly at the event
5. **Have backup options** available

## Timeline and Logistics

### Q: When should I send invitations?
A: Follow these timing guidelines:
- **Save-the-dates**: 6-8 months before (for destination events)
- **Formal invitations**: 6-8 weeks before
- **RSVP deadline**: 2-3 weeks before the event
- **Final headcount**: 1 week before the event

### Q: What should be included in my event timeline?
A: Create a detailed timeline including:
- **Setup time**: When vendors arrive and setup begins
- **Guest arrival**: Welcome and registration process
- **Program schedule**: Speeches, activities, entertainment
- **Meal service**: Cocktails, dinner, dessert timing
- **Breakdown**: When cleanup and vendor departure occurs

## Vendor Management

### Q: How many vendor quotes should I get?
A: Get at least 3 quotes for major services to:
- Compare pricing and packages
- Understand market rates
- Negotiate better terms
- Have backup options if needed

### Q: What questions should I ask potential vendors?
A: Key questions for all vendors:
- Are you available on my event date?
- What's included in your package?
- What are your payment terms?
- Do you have liability insurance?
- Can you provide recent client references?
- What's your cancellation policy?
- Do you have backup equipment/staff?

## Day-of Event Management

### Q: Should I hire a day-of coordinator?
A: Consider hiring a coordinator if:
- You have a large or complex event
- You want to enjoy your own event
- You have multiple vendors to manage
- You lack experience in event management
- Your budget allows for this service

### Q: What should I have in my emergency kit?
A: Pack these essentials:
- First aid supplies
- Stain removal pens
- Safety pins and tape
- Phone chargers and extension cords
- Cash for tips and emergencies
- Vendor contact list
- Event timeline and floor plan
- Backup decorations and supplies

## Post-Event

### Q: What should I do after the event?
A: Complete these post-event tasks:
1. **Thank your vendors** and provide feedback
2. **Send thank you notes** to key participants
3. **Review your budget** and actual expenses
4. **Collect feedback** from attendees
5. **Document lessons learned** for future events
6. **Handle final payments** and vendor settlements
7. **Store or return** rented items and decorations
